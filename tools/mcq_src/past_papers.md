## Free Radicals

### T/F

#### Free radicals are removed by `DU-24N`
- a) Glutathione — **True.** Glutathione peroxidase converts H₂O₂ to water.
- b) NADPH oxidase — **False.** Generates superoxide (ROS); does not remove it.
- c) Ceruloplasmin — **True.** Binds copper, preventing ROS-generating reactions (as do transferrin, ferritin, lactoferrin).
- d) Vit. D — **False.** Not an antioxidant (vitamins A, C, E are).
- e) Superoxide dismutase — **True.** Converts superoxide to H₂O₂.

## Apoptosis

### T/F

#### Morphological changes of apoptosis include `May 2025`
- a) Cellular swelling — **False.** Cell shrinkage; swelling is necrosis.
- b) Intact membrane — **True per printed key** (key's note: "membrane is disrupted in necrosis"). Plasma membrane stays intact in apoptosis.
- c) Membrane blebs formation — **True.** Extensive surface blebbing.
- d) Nuclear pyknosis — **False per key** (listed as feature of necrosis). ⚠ Key note: pyknosis can also occur in apoptosis, but key marks False.
- e) Nuclear fragmentation — **True.** Chromatin condenses peripherally; nucleus breaks into fragments — the most characteristic feature.

#### Characteristics of apoptosis include `DU-22N`
- a) Cellular swelling — **False.** Shrinkage; swelling is necrosis.
- b) Chromatin condensation — **True.** Most characteristic feature.
- c) Intact membrane — **True.** (Membrane is disrupted in necrosis.)
- d) Pyknosis — **False per key.** Listed as a feature of necrosis.
- e) Inflammation — **False.** Adjacent inflammation is frequent in necrosis, not apoptosis.

#### Morphological changes of apoptosis include `DU-21M`
- a) Nuclear pyknosis — **False per key** (feature of necrosis).
- b) Nuclear fragmentation — **True.** Most characteristic feature.
- c) Cellular swelling — **False.** Cell shrinkage in apoptosis.
- d) Intact membrane — **True.** Disrupted in necrosis.
- e) Membrane blebs formation — **True.** Extensive surface blebbing.

#### Apoptosis is characterized by `DU-19Nov`
- a) Cellular swelling — **False.** Shrinkage.
- b) Chromatin fragmentation — **True.** Hallmark.
- c) Membrane intact — **True.** Membrane preserved.
- d) Nuclear karyolysis — **False.** Karyolysis is a necrosis pattern.
- e) Inflammation — **True per printed key.** ⚠ Key note: apoptosis classically does NOT provoke inflammation; this key appears erroneous.

## Adaptations: Metaplasia and Hyperplasia

### T/F

#### Metaplasia is characterized by `DU-23N`
- a) Irreversible change — **False.** Reversible.
- b) Multipotent stem cell origin — **False per key.** Transformation of one differentiated cell type to another (via stem-cell reprogramming), not directly from multipotent stem cells.
- c) Involvement of epithelial cells only — **False.** Epithelial (squamous, columnar) and mesenchymal (osseous, cartilaginous).
- d) Fertile soil for malignancy — **True.**
- e) Vitamin-A deficiency — **False per key.** (Vit A deficiency is a cause of squamous metaplasia, but key marks False as a "characteristic".)

#### Hyperplasia is related to `DU-22M`
- a) Enlargement of cells — **False.** Increase in cell number (enlargement = hypertrophy).
- b) Tripolar mitosis — **False.** Abnormal mitosis occurs in dysplasia/neoplasia.
- c) Future neoplasia — **True.** Hyperplasia and metaplasia are fertile soil for cancer.
- d) Increased hormonal influence — **True.** e.g., proliferation of glandular elements of female breast.
- e) Normal volume of cells — **False.** Increase in cell number.

#### Examples of physiological hyperplasia include `DU-20N/M`
- a) Breast in puberty — **True.** Hormonal physiological hyperplasia.
- b) Endometrial hyperplasia after menopause — **False.** Pathological hyperplasia.
- c) Nodular goitre — **False.** Pathological.
- d) Benign prostatic hyperplasia — **False.** Pathological hyperplasia.
- e) Uterus during pregnancy — **True.** Hormonal physiological hyperplasia (with hypertrophy).

# Inflammation and Repair

## Acute and Chronic Inflammation

### T/F

#### Acute inflammation is associated with `DU-23M`
- a) Vasodilation — **True.** Early vascular change.
- b) Lymphocytes infiltration — **False.** Neutrophilic infiltration is characteristic of acute inflammation.
- c) Tissue destruction — **False.** A feature of chronic inflammation.
- d) Increased vascular permeability — **True.** Causes exudation.
- e) Phagocytosis — **True.** Neutrophils/macrophages ingest microbes.

#### Features associated with chronic inflammation `DU-21M`
- a) Active inflammation — **True.** Ongoing inflammation with tissue injury and repair together.
- b) Neutrophils inflammation — **False.** Neutrophil is the acute inflammatory cell.
- c) Tissue destruction — **True.** Hallmark.
- d) Persist for an hour — **False.** Persists weeks to months.
- e) Complete resolution — **False.** Healing is by fibrosis.

#### Features of acute inflammation include `DU-19Nov`
- a) Complete resolution — **True.** One outcome of acute inflammation.
- b) Lymphocytes predominance — **True per printed key.** ⚠ Key note: neutrophils predominate in acute inflammation.
- c) Healing by fibrosis — **True.** Outcome when tissue destruction is substantial.
- d) Progression to chronic inflammation — **True.** Possible outcome.
- e) Exudative oedema — **True.** Increased permeability → protein-rich exudate.

#### Exudate is associated with `DU-20N/M`
- a) Low protein content — **False.** High protein.
- b) Acute inflammation — **True.** Inflammatory fluid.
- c) Low specific gravity — **False.** High (>1.020).
- d) Increased tendency to clot — **True.** Due to high fibrinogen.
- e) High cellular content — **True.** Plenty of polymorphs.

## Edema

### T/F

#### Pitting oedema occurs in the following conditions `DU-23N`
- a) Filariasis — **False.** Non-pitting (lymphatic obstruction).
- b) Congestive cardiac failure — **True.** Fluid-rich pitting oedema.
- c) Following radiotherapy of breast cancer — **False.** Lymphoedema, non-pitting.
- d) Kwashiorkor — **False per printed key.** ⚠ Key note: kwashiorkor (hypoalbuminaemia) usually causes pitting oedema.

#### Examples of exudative oedema include `DU-21M`
- a) Oedema of congestive heart failure — **False.** Transudative.
- b) Tubercular pleural effusion — **True.** Inflammatory (exudate).
- c) Blisters of burn — **True.** Inflammatory exudate.
- d) PEM — **False.** Transudative (hypoproteinaemia).
- e) Ascites in cirrhosis — **False.** Transudative.

## Thrombosis

### T/F

#### Components of Virchow's triad include `May 2025`
- a) Stasis — **True.** Abnormal blood flow.
- b) Turbulence — **True.** Abnormal blood flow.
- c) Thrombosis — **False.** Result of the triad, not a component.
- d) Hypercoagulability — **True.**
- e) Inflammation — **False per key.** Not a triad component (it causes endothelial injury).

#### Components of a thrombus include `DU-24M`
- a) Platelets — **True.**
- b) Fat — **False.**
- c) Fibrin — **True.**
- d) White blood cells — **False per printed key.** ⚠ Key note: WBCs are normally trapped in thrombi.
- e) Marrow elements — **False.**

#### Characters noted in thrombus formation include `DU-22M`
- a) Platelet activation and release reaction in common — **True.**
- b) Endothelial damage is frequent — **True.** Most important factor.
- c) Coralline thrombus is mostly formed in venous channels — **True per key.** Fibrin/platelet laminae (lines of Zahn) form the ribbed appearance.
- d) Dissolution is an uncommon fate — **False.** Dissolution (fibrinolysis) is a common fate.
- e) Mostly composed of RBC and WBC — **False.** Platelets and fibrin predominate (arterial/mural).
